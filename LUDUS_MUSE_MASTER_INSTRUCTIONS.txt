# Master Specification — LUDUS / Professor D / AEGIS V2

## Identity and non-negotiables
- **LUDUS** is the platform and academy. **Professor D** is its Doctore: the teacher physically present in the scene. Do not title the product 'LUDUS Professor D.'
- He is a LIVE AI-powered, adaptive educator and an in-world guide, not a static mascot, a generic chatbot or a template lesson-plan generator.
- The core stage is a cinematic, original 2D/2.5D classroom and explorable-feeling topic scenes. They do not require a full navigable 3D world or Unreal Engine.
- Environments and objects must be *substantive teaching tools*: show a concept, respond to input, accurately change state, and support assessment.
- Support arbitrary safe topics through **composition from verified primitives and available assets**, not a claim of unlimited generative physics or custom worlds. Disclose unsupported cases.
- All development occurs through Muse in the existing Codespace, under AEGIS V2 reusable component and verification gates. Not GENESIS.

## UX core loop
1. Enter LUDUS Academy; Professor D welcomes student by name only if provided.
2. Student selects a topic and goal in natural language.
3. Professor D conducts adaptive **5–10-question** initial assessment (minimum 5 by default); question sequence adapts to answers, scope and prereqs. Offer skip/accessibility option and continue diagnosing during instruction. No universal one-size-fits-all fixed quiz.
4. Student receives a brief human-readable learning goal; full plan stays behind a disclosure panel, not center stage.
5. World Architect picks or composes an interactive lesson scene; Scene Director places Professor D and tools.
6. Professor D moves, points, demonstrates, asks for learner action, explains based on actual state and real model output.
7. Student manipulates objects and explains observations. The interaction is evidence for updating learner model.
8. Errors trigger changed explanation/representation and another attempt, not arbitrary 'Correct!'.
9. Exit test uses NEW problem; only then make evidence-based mastery claim; provide summary and next challenge.

## Ordering of success
**P0** Preserve functioning app, secure live AI and verify real lesson response; no breakage.
**P1** Canonical Professor D art, well-designed LUDUS Academy, meaningful walking/gesture animation and on-screen teaching surface.
**P1** Real adaptive assessment with actual evidence and topic-specific learner state.
**P1** Scene Director connects live reasoning to safe structured actions that render meaningfully.
**P1** At least one end-to-end new-topic lesson visually demonstrated in browser, with user-action, adaptation, and verification.
**P2** More rich scene art, topic packs, voice selection, sprite refinement, image generation on demand.
**P3** 3D worlds, autonomous arbitrary simulation generation, real-time lip sync and sophisticated character rigs.

## Hard acceptance
- Screenshot of app must immediately read as premium original academy, **not** white page with a few vector labels.
- Character must not be tiny stick figure or generic vector professor. Consistent character master and derived poses.
- AI lesson for 'how to build a castle' must teach real castle design tradeoffs with actual manipulable walls, gates, towers or clearly grounded supported parts; no generic 'find an everyday example' repeated filler.
- 'Clouds', 'fractions', 'animals', and an unfamiliar safe topic must generate appropriately differentiated scenes and explanations, with valid limitations.
- Real browser human/automated test, not unit tests alone; document if browser automation unavailable.

# Professor D Animation / Performance Contract

## Do not confuse 'character present' with 'animated educator'
Animation must communicate a teaching purpose. Character should walk to the object, face it, gesture at the relevant part, observe student input, react to assessment and return to idle. No meaningless constant looping or arbitrary success animations.

## Actions
- `enter(x,y)`, `moveTo(anchor,speed)`, `face(target)`, `point(target)`, `gesture(kind)`, `present(target)`, `listen()`, `think()`, `encourage()`, `celebrateAfterVerifiedCorrect()`, `idle()`.
- Each action has a schema, coordinates bounded to safe stage, optional duration, and declared fallback. Character motion/state managed by one coordinator to avoid contradictory simultaneous actions.
- Scene object anchors are typed names/IDs, never freeform CSS injected by AI.
- Arrival/gesture sequences align with spoken text or onscreen caption; navigation cannot obscure the educational object or text.

## Execution realism
Use layered 2D/2.5D sprites, sprite atlases, tweening, CSS/Canvas or a carefully chosen animation library. A full motion-capture rig is not required. Prioritize high-quality walk cycle, turn, point, idle and thoughtful expressions. Keep fps, memory and bundle size reasonable. Provide reduced-motion fallback (static states, no forced pans) and keyboard equivalents.

## Proof
Show in-browser: intro walk -> stop at teaching wall -> point to fraction object -> explain -> student moves object -> listen/thinking -> targeted response. Record screenshot sequence or reproducible manual test. Tests inspect state changes, but founder visually judges naturalness and art quality.

## FILE: 04_WORLD_ENGINE.md

# Dynamic 2D World Engine / AI Scene Director

## Architecture
- AI **Learning Environment Architect**: interprets objective, student evidence, prerequisites; selects pedagogy and scene type; determines desired observable student actions.
- AI **Scene Director**: outputs a structured scene *plan* and a list of allowlisted typed actions, referring to scene graph IDs.
- Trusted **World Renderer**: validates and runs the plan; never evals AI-generated JS or lets AI freely fetch/execute code. Reuse registry components where possible.
- Deterministic **Simulations**: math, units, and scientific causal relationships must be coded/tested, not guessed from AI art.
- **Asset Manager**: selects approved sprites, layered backgrounds, procedurally rendered shapes and optional model-generated imagery. Maintain style/provenance/cache.
- **Learner Model**: reports observed interactions and understanding back to Director; never infer mastery from a flashy animation.

## Minimum typed commands
`createObject(type, properties, location)`, `compose(objects, relationships)`, `show/hide`, `move/rotate/scale`, `highlight`, `annotate`, `transitionScene`, `cameraFocus`, `playValidatedSimulation`, `startInteraction`, `observeInteraction`, `characterAction`.
Define schemas, permissions, coordinate constraints, units, collision/placement if necessary, runtime failures, and safe fallback.

## Reusable primitives
- Geometric/fraction shapes, tiling/masking, rulers and number lines
- Draggable blocks/bricks/walls, hinges, gates, spans, levers, labels and arrows
- Weather layers, solar heat, vapor, condensation, cloud categories, gauges
- Animal/habitat sprites, comparison panels, anatomical labels, interactive hotspots
- Timelines, maps, plots, diagrams and structured callouts
- Text/image panel, validated question widget, code editor/simulator hooks

## Topic proof scenarios
**Castle:** Learner assembles wall segments, towers and gate placement for a medieval castle, learns defensive design, material/structural constraints and tradeoffs. Objects must actually drag/connect or change, not just display named rectangles. No invented historical universals.
**Fractions:** Pizza/blocks show equal partitions with exact mathematics, equivalence, compare/compose, learner can manipulate fractions and get checked state.
**Clouds:** Interact with temperature/humidity simplified condensation model, different cloud types and correct explanation of rising air/cooling; explicitly label simplified physical model.
**Animals:** Habitat and real animal visuals with factual labels and meaningful classification/adaptation interaction; art should not be used as proof of biology fact.
**Unfamiliar topic:** Build a distinct scene by composing already-verified primitives or use an explicit limited fallback. Do not hardcode the unfamiliar test.

## Scene and asset hierarchy
High fidelity scenic art behind programmatic hit targets. Rich background can be an image layer; teachable dynamic objects must be discrete, independently manipulable, labeled through accessible UI, and track their state. Never force generic generated image to pretend to be a physical simulation.

## Reuse and testing
Each world component has version, contract, asset dependencies, input/output events, security requirements, tests and known supported domains. AEGIS V2 gates validate plans and tool selection. Browser test verifies visible world render, action, student manipulation, targeted adaptation and correct results. State performance limits and cache/budget behavior.

## FILE: 05_TEACHING_INTELLIGENCE.md

# Professor D — Adaptive Teaching & Diagnostics

## Student assessment
Default **5–10 genuinely informative questions** before the primary lesson. Count may vary by domain breadth, student's performance and objective. Normally five minimum; avoid forced exhaustion for accessibility or skip requests. Offer deeper optional diagnosis for broad expertise. Make initial diagnosis engaging and integrated with visual tasks.
- Elicit precise learning goal, prior background, desired challenge and accessibility constraints without interrogating.
- Use dependency map / prereq concepts, not three generic memorized prompts.
- Questions can be oral/written, multiple-choice with meaningful distractors, short explanation, prediction, number work, visual location or manipulations; grade by real evidence.
- Adaptive: start calibrated; escalate sophistication for correct answers; isolate incorrect reasoning with discriminating questions; avoid duplicating near-equivalent questions.
- Track item skill, difficulty, observed answer, correctness, explanation evidence, confidence/uncertainty, misconception hypothesis and evidence, next question choice.
- Separate fact/understanding/transfer; high confidence is not mastery. Stop only when sufficient evidence for the requested lesson starting point (not absolute topic mastery).

## Teaching loop
`diagnose -> choose objective -> choose method & scene -> demonstrate -> interact -> observe -> diagnose misconception -> change representation -> practice -> transfer-test -> summary`.
- Use live Meta AI for novel explanations and adaptive teaching, but ground factual/verifiable tasks in validated materials or deterministic checkers. Do not use generic filler like 'think of one everyday example of the topic'.
- Explain castle construction with actual castle construction content; cloud formation with real atmospheric process; animals with correct biology; fractions with exact pieces.
- The interface should show the learning experience by default; diagnostic report and multi-step plan only in collapsible side panels.
- Feedback must cite actual student action, provide an actionable hint, and avoid fake applause.
- Allow the student to ask unrelated clarifications, switch subject and receive an honest unsupported-tool fallback when needed.

## Safety
Age appropriate interactions, child privacy consideration, accessibility, avoid unsupported medical/physical danger advice, safe learning sandbox for engineering. Do not store identifiable student data or cross-learner memory without consent and policy review.

## Acceptance tests
Five distinct learner archetypes (novice, intermediate, expert, one misconception, inconsistent answers) must yield measurably different questions, knowledge map, chosen scene/task and follow-up instructions. Test one arbitrary safe unseen topic and validate that real curriculum is delivered, not a template. A 5–10 question count alone is insufficient evidence of quality.
